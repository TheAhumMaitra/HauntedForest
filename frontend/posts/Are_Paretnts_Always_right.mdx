# Can an ordinary human being be right on every aspect?
It is notable that humans aren't infallible, an ordinary human being cannot be infallible (can't do any mistake) in every moment of his life; famous respected philosopher Aristotle says in his book - _Nicomachean Ethics_, Book I, Chapter 3, 1094b27:-

<Epigraph
  quote="ἕκαστος δὲ κρίνει καλῶς ἃ γινώσκει, καὶ τούτων ἐστὶν ἀγαθὸς κριτής."
  author="Aristotle"
  source="Nicomachean Ethics, Book I, Chapter 3, 1094b27"
/>

if we translate, it becomes :-

<Epigraph
  quote="Each man judges correctly those matters with which he is acquainted; it is of these that he is a competent critic."
  author="Translator - W. D. Ross translation, original text by Aristotle"
  source="Nicomachean Ethics, Book I, Chapter 3, 1094b27"
/>

the philosopher means :-

1. **A person can judge correctly in matters they understand**
2. **That does mean that they qualified to judge in every subject**
3. **Expertise and knowledge matter when evaluating whether something is true.**

Conclusion is that, merely being a human being does not make someone an authoritative judge of every subject.

Aristotle also says in his book - _Metaphysics_, Book II (α), Chapter 1, 993b :-

<Epigraph
  quote="ἀλλ᾽ ἕκαστον λέγειν τι περὶ τῆς φύσεως, καὶ καθ᾽ ἕνα μὲν ἢ μηθὲν ἢ μικρὸν ἐπιβάλλειν αὐτῇ, ἐκ πάντων δὲ συναθροιζομένων γίγνεσθαί τι μέγεθος."
  author="Aristotle"
  source="Metaphysics, Book II (α), Chapter 1, 993b"
/>

if we translate, that becomes :-

<Epigraph
  quote="Each thinker makes some statement about the natural world, and as an individual contributes little or nothing to the inquiry; but a combination of all conjectures results in something considerable"
  author="Translator - W. D. Ross translation, original text by Aristotle"
  source="Metaphysics, Book II (α), Chapter 1, 993b"
/>

he means :-

1. **An individual human being does not necessarily possess the whole truth**

2. **Individual thinkers may contribute only a little to understanding reality**

3. **Different thinkers collectively contribute to a greater understanding of truth**

Conclusion, Aristotle is essentially acknowledging the limitations of individual human knowledge.

So we now know in simple words that according to Aristotle, a human cannot be fully knowledgeable in every subject and also that being a human does not mean that we are qualified to judge everything, so humans can't be infallible over every judgement in history, that would be mean that every judgement in our life by us is also not right every single time. Same rule for all humans.

_**Note :**_ A human can be infallible over certain thing but not everything

Respected Theist Philosopher and theologian Saint Thomas Aquinas says in his ultimate book - _Summa Theologiae_, Part 1, Question. 85, Article. 6 :-

<Epigraph
  quote="Intellectus autem circa quidditatem rei semper verus est, sicut et sensus circa proprium sensibile, ut supra dictum est. Sed per accidens potest ibi esse falsitas, inquantum intellectus falso componit vel dividit."
  author="Saint Thomas Aquinas"
  source="Summa Theologiae, I, Q. 85, Art. 6"
/>

if we translate, that means :-

<Epigraph
  quote="The intellect is always true as regards what a thing is, just as sight is always true as regards its proper sensible object. But accidentally falsity can occur in the intellect, in so far as it falsely composes or divides."
  author="Translator - The Fathers of the English Dominican Province, original text by Aristotle"
  source="Summa Theologiae, I, Q. 85, Art. 6"
/>

The philosopher is distinguishing the difference between understanding what something is and passing a judgement over it


He also says in work _Respondeo of I_, Question. 85, Article. 6 :-

<Epigraph
  quote="Circa sensibilia vero communia decipitur sensus, sicut in diiudicando de magnitudine vel figura; ut cum iudicat solem esse pedalem, qui tamen est maior terra. Et multo magis decipitur circa sensibilia per accidens."
  author="Saint Thomas Aquinas"
  source="Respondeo of I, Q. 85, Art. 6."
/>

if we translate it becomes :-

<Epigraph
  quote="TBut with regard to those things which surround the essence or quiddity of a thing, the intellect can be mistaken, when it relates one thing to another, either by composing or dividing, or even by reasoning."
  author="Translator - The Fathers of the English Dominican Province, original text by Saint Thomas Aquinas"
  source="Respondeo of I, Q. 85, Art. 6."
/>

the philosopher acknowledging that human reasoning can lead to error, which is crucial.


So, we can conclude that humans can't be right over everything, human judgement in every subject isn't right and being a judge doesn't mean that you are qualified for it (ex: you can become a father but that doesn't mean you are able to be a father - causes example : financial stability, mental maturity, etc); also that human reasoning can lead to wrong results
