## Sidenotes and Margin Notes

The defining signature of a Tufte layout is the margin note column. Instead of burying references at the bottom of the page in distant footnotes, notes and commentary sit right beside the text they illuminate.<Sidenote>This is an automatically numbered sidenote. On wide screens, it sits peacefully in the right-hand margin. On mobile screens, tap the superscript number to reveal it inline.</Sidenote>

You can also use unnumbered margin notes for informal commentary or definitions.<MarginNote>Margin notes don't increment the note counter, providing a clean annotation style for parenthetical thoughts.</MarginNote> Readers can follow the core narrative without distraction or explore the margin for rich context.

## Mathematical Precision with KaTeX

Scientific and algorithmic thinking requires clear notation. Both inline equations such as $E = mc^2$ and Euler's formula $e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$ render smoothly alongside text.

For more complex derivations, display math blocks provide centered, high-clarity typography:

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2} \, dx = \sqrt{\pi}
$$

Or Gauss's Law for electromagnetism in differential form:

$$
\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}
$$
